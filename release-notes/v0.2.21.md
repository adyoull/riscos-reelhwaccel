Hardware video for RISC OS on the Raspberry Pi: H.264 decoding and encoding on the VideoCore, and HEVC decoding on the Pi 4's HEVC block, for FFmpeg and Reel.

New since v0.2.18: no 2 s wait when an H.264 decoder starts or seeks, a clean seek with direct output, and a shorter freeze if the VideoCore stops answering.

## No wait at the start

vcdec used to give the decoder its picture buffers only once the decoder had said the picture size, or after 2 s. That rule came from the first tests on the Pi, where a picture went missing. The real cause turned out to be something else: a buffer numbered 0, which the firmware takes as "no buffer".

Some streams only say their size once the decoder has buffers, so every start waited the whole 2 s. Clips made by `h264_encvchiq` are like this, and Reel Edit waited at every cut.

Now the buffers go to the decoder as soon as it's created, and again every time it's created afresh. On a Pi 4 (VCDecTest's Start) every picture of four clips was right, including a seek after the end and two 1080p decoders at once. The first picture came 1–5 cs after the first packet. `VCDEC_OUT_AFTER_EFCH` restores the old way.

## Seeking with direct output

With direct output (v0.2.18), a picture the decoder had already started could still arrive after a seek. VCDecTest's Reverse found one on a Pi 4. Now, with direct output, every seek (`avcodec_flush_buffers`) makes a new VideoCore decoder rather than flushing the old one, as vcdec already did after the end of a stream. On the Pi this took 1–3 cs, about as long as a flush, and the first picture came 3–7 cs later at 1080p. With bulk transfers vcdec still flushes, as before.

## A decoder that stops answering

Reel Edit once saw a decoder stop answering when it was closed, after many seeks backwards. The close then waited 300 cs for each of three steps, freezing the desktop for 9 s. VCDecTest's Reverse, with 45 seeks of that kind and the close timed each time, didn't reproduce it on our Pi.

If it happens, vcdec now gives up after the first step that gets no reply, so the wait is 3 s. It logs "The decoder stopped answering", and leaves the stuck decoder any buffers it still holds. Its VideoCore memory may stay taken until a restart.

## Also

- **The log** says when the decoder's format change came, and how many access units had been sent by then.
- **`vcdec_stats`** has the start's timings (`efch_cs`, `efch_aus`, `first_cs`, `efch_waits`).
- **Upgrading:** `vcdec_stats` is bigger again, so rebuild anything that uses it against the new headers.
